Method. Each week's forecast averages three signals: a least-squares trend line across the trailing 8 weeks, a recency-weighted average of the last 4 weeks (weights 1–2–3–4), and a seasonal projection — last year's matching fiscal week scaled by this year's aggregate year-over-year growth over the same 8-week window. The daily split uses each store's own average day-of-week share of weekly sales from the most recent 8 weeks (rolled forward to 8/3–9/27 as of 2026-09-29, previously frozen at 7/13–9/6), so Mon–Sun always sums exactly to the weekly forecast. The low–high range is the forecast ±1.28× the store's 8-week volatility (≈80% historical range) — a guide, not a guarantee. Source: Keys – Trend Costs Trend reports, weeks ending 2026-07-19 through 2026-09-06, plus the matching fiscal weeks from 2025.

Labor plan method. Ideal Labor $ and Hours Needed are each fit per store as a straight line against sales — Ideal Labor $ from one week of actual Ideal Labor % × Sales (week of 9/7–13), Hours Needed from one week of the company's own Forecast Sales vs. Hours Needed (All groups, week of 9/14–20, KEYS Labor Schedule report) — then applied to each week's forecasted sales. R² per store ranges 0.84–0.97 for Ideal Labor $ and 0.98–0.99 for Hours Needed (see workbook, "Labor Model Inputs" tab). Both fits rest on a single week of source data and should be re-fit as more weeks accumulate. Managers are salaried in the source labor-cost data, so Hours Needed is a total-labor-hours planning figure, not a direct wage-cost conversion.
